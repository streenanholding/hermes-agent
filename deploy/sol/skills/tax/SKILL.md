## Standing positions to verify each year (rules change; check current law before relying on these)
- **R&D credit (IRC §41).** Qualified research must be done **in the United States** (§41(d)(4)(F)). Mango IT's development work is performed in India, so it is **not** a qualified research expense for the federal credit. Don't count it. U.S.-based items that can count: wages of U.S. employees doing qualified research, U.S. contractors (65% of cost), and cloud computing used for development and testing (not production hosting). Rick's and Nancy's own time counts only if they are paid wages.
- **Payroll-tax offset:** a qualified small business (under $5M gross receipts, within its first 5 years with gross receipts) can apply up to $500,000/year of the R&D credit against payroll taxes, but only if FinVerified has payroll. With no payroll, the credit carries forward. Flag this to Rick.
- **Research expenses (§174 / §174A).** Under current law, domestic research expenses can be deducted right away. Foreign research expenses, including Mango's India-based development, are amortized over 15 years. Confirm with the CPA; this materially changes 2026 taxable income.
- **Foreign contractor:** collect a W-8BEN-E from Mango IT Solutions; no 1099. Confirm no U.S. withholding applies (services performed outside the U.S.).
- **1099-NEC:** U.S. contractors paid $600+ (check the current threshold). Collect W-9s now, not in January.
- **Delaware franchise tax and annual report** (due March 1): compute under the Assumed Par Value Capital method, which is usually far cheaper for startups than the default bill.
- **Florida:** confirm whether the Florida corporate income tax filing and sales tax on SaaS (generally not taxable in Florida) apply.
- **Form D** within 15 days of the first SAFE sale; state blue-sky notices. Coordinate with counsel.
- **409A valuation** before any option grants.
