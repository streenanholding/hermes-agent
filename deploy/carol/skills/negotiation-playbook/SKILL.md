# Negotiation playbook

## Goal
The lowest total cost in the market **and** the best service for Pay✓erified: speed,
accuracy, coverage, support and security. Cheap but unreliable loses; reliable but
expensive loses. Win both.

## Talk about our volume ambitiously — and truthfully
- Present the opportunity big: Fin✓erified is onboarding practices nationally; Pay✓erified
  is designed for national, high-volume use across dental, medical and hospital practices,
  every visit, every payment. Use the RFP's volume bands and our growth plan.
- Always frame future volume as a **plan, forecast or target** ("we're planning for…",
  "our target by end of 2027 is…"). Never state a forecast as current fact, never
  invent customer counts, logos, signed deals or other bidders' prices.
- Turn ambition into price: ask for **band pricing that steps down automatically** as
  volume arrives. That lets the vendor price to our future without us promising it,
  and gets us the best rate the moment we earn it.

## Levers to ask for (in this order of value)
1. Lowest unit price at Bands C and D, with automatic step-downs and the yearly price cut.
2. No minimums, no setup fees, free sandbox, no charge for failed or duplicate transactions.
3. Most-favored pricing: no similar customer pays less.
4. Service levels with money behind them: availability, p95 response time, payer
   enrollment turnaround, support response times, incident notice — with service credits.
5. A named account lead and engineering contact; quarterly business reviews.
6. Savings share (50/50 for 12 months) on cost ideas they bring.
7. Price protection and a ramp period; termination for convenience.

## Running the process
- **Competitive tension, honestly:** say there are several strong bidders and we will award
  on price and quality. Never reveal or invent another bidder's numbers.
- **Anchor with the market:** quote public list prices or published benchmarks with sources.
- **Bundle trades:** give what is cheap for us (case study after launch, reference, faster
  decision, longer term) for what is valuable (price, credits, support).
- **Every concession gets something back.** Write it down in the vendor's tracker.
- **Best and final:** before the award, ask the top two for their best and final offer by a
  fixed deadline, in writing.
- **Walk-away:** know it before every call — the price and service level where we pick the
  next vendor instead. Rick and Nancy approve the walk-away.

## Service quality check before the award
Run the RFP's synthetic test set in the sandbox: response time, completeness of benefit
detail, error rates, enrollment steps, documentation quality, how fast support answers a
real question. Score it; it can move a finalist up or down.

## What you never do
Accept terms, sign, promise volume, promise the award, share another bidder's proposal,
disclose confidential Pay✓erified details beyond the RFP without an NDA, or misstate facts.
You recommend; Rick and Nancy decide.
